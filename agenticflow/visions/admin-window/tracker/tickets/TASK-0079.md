# Designer's lane: record Ben's two LOOK_AND_FEEL rulings — a bar against controls at the bottom of a long list, and the paging clause on bar 11

## Description
Two rulings from Ben, both edits to the document the designer is sole writer of, both filed verbatim by the dispatcher after he walked /claims paging on his own instance on 2026-09-11 (inbox note 2026-09-11-ben-paging-rulings.md, sections 2 and 3). LOOK_AND_FEEL is human-owned and neither the designer nor the architect may invent or retire a bar — M3.md says so twice. These two are Ben's words, so the authority is in hand and this is transcription plus shaping, not taste.

RULING A — a new Feel bar, section 2, his words:

    "There should not be elements at the bottom of a list this long. We should put them at the top or on a different page, depending on the importance. The elements that show up at the bottom of this page after the list are extremely easy to not be able to find because I wouldn't think to scroll to the bottom of the list to find more elements."

This REPLACES the bar the designer proposed at the M3 early walk and re-proposed in the endgame report — "a control that extends a list is still on screen after it acts" (M3.md, "For Ben" item 1). Ben's version is stronger and differently shaped: it is not about the control staying on screen after it acts, it is about the control never being at the bottom in the first place. Write Ben's, retire the proposal, and say in the M3 record that the proposal is closed by his ruling so the next walker does not re-propose it.

RULING B — one clause on bar 11, section 3, his ruling: add a clause naming paging as the exception to "state lives in the URL" and naming what stands in for the URL. Bar 11 is currently graded against ARCHITECTURE's dated 2026-09-10 paging amendment rather than against its own sentence, and every walk that touches it has to narrate that (M3 endgame walk, bar 11: "bar is STALE, app is right — no ticket"). The clause ends that limbo.

THE SHAPE QUESTION, AND WHY THIS TICKET DOES NOT RESOLVE IT. Ben's section 1 ruling — page windows of an adjustable size (20 / 50 / 100), items 1-50 / 51-100, replacing append-on-press on both paged surfaces — is milestone-sized and has been STOPPED for his decision (see the strategist's digest entry, 2026-09-11). If he adopts it, page position may well live in the URL as ?page= / ?size=, which would make bar 11 TRUE again rather than excepted, and the clause would be deleted rather than amended. So: write the clause as a statement about the paging state model AS IT SHIPS TODAY, dated, and say in the clause itself that it is retired if page position ever enters the URL. Do not write a clause that would survive a shape change it was never about. The note's own words: "the designer and architect decide the shape together" — so if the clause cannot be written honestly without knowing the shape, say so on the ticket and stop rather than guessing.

WHAT THIS TICKET MUST NOT DO: file the consequence. Ruling A's bar is failed today by /claims (the control goes viewport y=434 to 2,066 with scrollY unchanged, 1,166px below a 900px fold — measured on the landed tree at 1440x900 in the M3 endgame walk) while /browse passes at 852 by scroll-anchoring accident. The FIX for that failure is the page-window redesign that is stopped for Ben, so filing a BUG against /claims now would hand a builder a ticket whose right answer depends on a decision nobody has made. Record the failing measurement in the bar's own record, file nothing against src/, and let Ben's ruling-1 decision pick the fix.
