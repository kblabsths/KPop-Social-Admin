# The window line says "the window did not fill — 30 of at most 50" over 50 rows on screen — the didNotFill clause fires when the COUNT read came back SMALLER than the rows

## Description
**Module:** `src/components/ui/window-line.tsx:517` — `didNotFill(info, rows)`, read by the `matched` arm's first-screen branch (`window-line.tsx:823`, `info.truncated || pageable(info) ? holdsBack : didNotFill(...)`) on the UNPAGED arm of /claims. Its gate is `info.truncated`, which on /claims is `listCount.data > listed.length` (`src/app/claims/page.tsx:1163`) — so a COUNT that comes back SMALLER than the rows drawn turns the gate OFF and renders the did-not-fill sentence over a screen that holds MORE rows than the number that sentence names.

**Expected** (the rule admin-window/BUG-0174 and admin-window/BUG-0183 closed on the clause next door, LESSONS 2, ARCHITECTURE §4.3): a clause that describes the screen states a number a read established about THAT screen, and a window that drew its whole cap never says it did not fill. BUG-0183 gave the held-back clause `drawn`; this branch of the same `if` still names `held` alone and asserts completeness over it.

**Found** (measured on run/admin-window at d4352b2 — the landed BUG-0183 tree — offline, through the page's real render from the real deps, fixture `offGridScript(rows, count)` in tests/offline/claims/page.test.ts):

- 50 rows drawn, count 30 -> `data-window-held=\"30\"`, `data-window-truncated=\"false\"`, no paging element, 50 claim rows in the markup, line: '… A window of at most 50 rows, not the whole view. The window did not fill — 30 of at most 50 — so it holds all the claims the read found.' The window DID fill: it drew 50 of its cap of 50.
- 37 rows drawn, count 30 -> the same sentence, byte for byte, over a screen holding 37.
- So two screens holding 50 and 37 claims tell the operator the same thing, and both are told the window holds all the claims the read found while the number in the sentence is smaller than the rows they can count.

Byte-identical before and after BUG-0183 (measured: the whole page markup for both fixtures is identical on f9e23f9 and on the landed tree), so this is NOT a regression from that ticket — it is the other direction of the same two-reads divergence, left standing because BUG-0183's criteria scoped `didNotFill` out for the AGREEING state ('must keep rendering exactly what it renders') and no criterion covered the disagreeing one.

**Reachability:** /claims issues the count and the window as TWO reads of `pending_claims`. The campaign has recorded the count moving under it twice (877 -> 878, admin-window/BUG-0174 and admin-window/BUG-0180); the resolver APPLYING a pending claim between the two reads moves it the other way, and any such move below `listed.length` lands here. Not reproduced on staging (its two reads agree today: 877 claims, 50 rows, walked in a browser on a production build on 2026-09-11).

**Repro (exact), offline:**
1. `./node_modules/.bin/vitest run --project=offline tests/offline/claims/page.test.ts -t \"COUNT read came back SMALLER\"`
2. The case (QA, in `describe(\"the claim list's window\")`) renders two screens under ONE count — 50 rows and 37 rows, both count 30 — and compares the two LINES against each other. It pins no word of the sentence. Watched RED on the landed tree at d4352b2 with every non-vacuity assertion green (50 and 37 rows drawn, held=30 and truncated=false on both, `pagingOccurrences` 0 on both).
3. Landed as a strict `it.fails` pin (this repo's QA convention), so the suite is green today and turns RED the moment the divergence disappears. **The fix also flips the pin** to a plain `it(...)`, which is what check 3 below measures (the pin was RETITLED after filing: its first clause was a prefix of admin-window/BUG-0183's own absence check and reddened it).

**NOT IN SCOPE:** the held-back clause and every criterion of admin-window/BUG-0183 (graded green on this tree, re-measured byte for byte); `didNotFill` where the two reads AGREE, which must keep rendering exactly what it renders; the PAGED arm, which cannot reach this branch (`pageable` keeps `didNotFill` off it, admin-window/BUG-0172); the empty-read case (`held === 0`), which is its own sentence; `src/components/ui/paging.tsx`. Whether the page should call this window truncated at all — i.e. whether the gate or the clause is the wrong half — is the fix shape, and it is the architect's to rule: the WORDS are the designer's, what is graded here is that a clause describing the screen names a number about the screen.

## Acceptance criteria
**The ruling (fix shape, architect 2026-09-11).** The matched arm's fill verdict comes from the ROW read against its own cap and from nothing else: `didNotFill` may render only where the window's own rows fell short of that cap — `drawn < limit`, which `drawn` states since admin-window/BUG-0183 — AND where the two reads agree (`readsAgree`, the one derivation). Where the rows reached the cap, or where the count and the rows disagree, each read is stated as its own fact — the count's number as the count's, the rows below as the rows' — and no clause asserts a relationship no single read established: no ranking of one out of the other, no "the rest are not shown", no claim that the window holds everything the read found. The decision is ONE named predicate in `src/components/ui/window-line.tsx`, derived from facts the window already carries (`drawn`, `limit`, `held`, `truncated`, `continues`); `DrawnWindow` gains no field and no call site passes a new prop. `/claims` composes exactly the window it composes today (`held: listCount.data`, `heldFrom: "a count read"`, `truncated` from the count, `drawn: listed.length`, `continues: pageable`) — the contract admin-window/TASK-0070, TASK-0071 and TASK-0072 build on does not move. The WORDS are the designer's: reuse this file's existing clause vocabulary where it fits and add at most one new clause, for a window that reached its cap.

Measured on the pre-ticket tree (a988b00) through the real component, unpaged matched arm, cap 50, `scope: null` (evidence/BUG-0186/probe.test.tsx): (drawn 50, count 30), (49, 30), (37, 30), (30, 30) and (50, 50) all render the SAME sentence, "The window did not fill — 30 of at most 50 — so it holds all the claims the read found" (with 50/50 for the last), so a screen holding 50 and a screen holding 30 are described identically and a window that drew its whole cap says it did not fill.
