# The /claims critical path: the pending-claims gauge stops being a four-deep read

## Description
**Module:** `src/lib/gauges/pending-claims.ts` (`fetchPendingClaims`) and `src/lib/db/claims.ts` (the reads it issues) — the pending-claims gauge, consumed by `/claims`, `/sources` (`readAwaitingRowTrend`) and the review item. ARCHITECTURE §4.3 read kind 2 (the two-step join). SHARED surface, so §13.2 applies and the offline checks below are wider than one file.

**Why this ticket exists.** TASK-0062 made `readRowsByIds` a bounded fan-out and MISSED M3 EC9's page bar honestly: warm `/claims` went 2.280 s -> 1.714 s median, and 1.4 s is the bar. That ticket is graded on its function; this ticket owns the page. **The milestone's bar is not lowered and not moved — it is re-homed here.**

**Read TASK-0062's census before you plan** (`agenticflow/tracker/evidence/TASK-0062/warm-times.md`): 23 PostgREST round trips on a bare `/claims`, 9 of them this gauge's second leg.

**The census decomposes exactly, and it says something the summary did not.** `/claims` composes its reads in ONE `Promise.all` at depth 1 (`src/app/claims/page.tsx`, "nothing here is sequenced"). Its 23 trips are: 1 window read + 1 list count + 5 per-bucket counts + 5 per-bucket oldest seeks + 1 source registry + **10 for the gauge** (1 `readPendingObservations`, then 9 chunk reads of `pending_claims`). 1+1+5+5+1+10 = 23. Every one of the 13 non-gauge siblings is a SINGLE round trip issued concurrently, so they cost `max`, not `sum`. **The remaining cost is not the 14 other legs — it is DEPTH in one leg.** `fetchPendingClaims` awaits the observations read, then feeds 877 ids into the fan-out: after TASK-0062 that is 1 + 3 = **four sequential waits**, and at the 0.14–1.4 s per trip that evidence note measures, four waits IS the 1.7 s. Nothing else on the page is deeper than one.

**The remedy is ruled in order, not left open.**

1. **First, prove or disprove the collapse.** The second leg exists because the gauge "windows on `observations`, the side that carries the timestamp" — but `pending_claims` carries `observed_at` too (`CLAIM_COLUMNS` selects it, and `readClaimCountSince(bounds.since, …)` already windows the claims side by it on this very page). If a bounded read of `pending_claims` over the SAME `resolveBounds` interval and the same filter returns the same claim set the id-list join returns, the two legs become concurrent siblings and the page's depth goes 4 -> 1. **Prove it, never assume it**: an offline fixture where the two shapes could disagree (a claim whose `observed_at` sits outside the window its observation is inside, and one where they agree), plus a live assertion on staging that both shapes produce identical aggregates over the real window. The observations leg still runs — `aggregatePendingClaims` needs the observation rows and the window line's denominator — it just stops being a prerequisite.
2. **If the sets are NOT equal, say so in the evidence note and take the measured fallback**: raise `CHUNK_FANOUT` (`src/lib/db/result.ts`) to at most 8, with before/after timings for each value you tried in the note. This lever was correctly refused by TASK-0062 as a way to dodge its bar; here it is a legitimate, measured choice about how much concurrency this database tolerates. It is the fallback, not the opening move, because it keeps the depth and only shortens it.
3. **Explicitly OUT of scope: the 10 per-bucket counts and seeks.** They are concurrent siblings; collapsing them into grouped reads buys DB load, not wall clock, and would put a rendered-figure rewrite in a latency ticket. Do not touch them. Also out of scope: paging (`FEAT-0015`), `ReadBounds` both-edges work (TASK-0070), and any rendered text anywhere.

**Refusal-shaped brief (LESSONS 10, common violations row 19).** You are rewriting a read that can refuse, and refusals are the way this goes wrong. A missing `observations` table and a missing `pending_claims` table must each still reach the page as `not_provisioned` naming THAT table — and once the legs are concurrent, "which refusal wins" is a decision you must pin, not discover: the OBSERVATIONS refusal wins, because that is what the sequential shape returned first today. A refusal on either side never becomes a half-filled gauge or a zero.

**The measurement (M3 EC9's third bullet, now owed here).** Warm server time over HTTP against a PRODUCTION build, the launch block in `agenticflow/docs/STACK.md` §5 verbatim with the env prefix on BOTH commands — before/after pair, same session, exact commands recorded in `agenticflow/tracker/evidence/<this ticket>/leg-times.md`. Four routes in the same pass: `/claims`, `/sources`, `/queues` and one review item. Include a per-leg census: the round trips each page issues, and the DEPTH (how many sequential waits) of the longest chain. If STACK.md's port is already bound, say which port you used and why, as TASK-0062 did.

**The bar: `/claims` warm at or under 1.4 s.** If the change lands and the bar is still missed, record the number and say so — never move the bar, never quietly widen the scope, and hand it back to the architect with the per-leg census. On a page whose census shows zero touched round trips, before/after sample ranges that overlap are jitter, not a regression.

## Acceptance criteria
The pending-claims gauge no longer waits on its own observations read before it can start reading claims: on a stub recording issue order, the observations read and the claims read are in flight at the same time, and the same test fails against the sequential shape (write it so it does, and say so in the test name). The claim set is UNCHANGED, proved on its own fixtures rather than assumed: a fixture where a windowed claims read and the id-list join could disagree (a claim whose observed_at sits outside the window its observation is inside) and one where they agree, plus a live check on staging that both shapes produce identical pending-claims aggregates over the real window; if they are not identical, the fallback in the description is taken instead and the note says so. Refusals survive concurrency and are pinned, not discovered: a missing observations table and a missing pending_claims table each still reach /claims, /sources and the review item as not_provisioned naming THAT table, an observations refusal is the one returned when both legs refuse, and no refusal on either side yields a half-filled gauge or a zero. Nothing rendered changes on /claims, /sources, /queues or the review item - their offline page suites and their live parity suites are green unchanged, and the per-bucket counts and seeks are untouched. (human-checkable) /claims warm server time over HTTP against a production build is at or under 1.4 s, measured as a before/after pair with the exact commands, with /sources, /queues and one review item measured in the same pass and none regressing beyond overlapping sample ranges; the measurement carries a per-leg census giving each page round-trip count and the DEPTH of its longest sequential chain. HOW THIS IS GRADED, because it cannot be a stored check: write the full artifact (leg-times.md) under agenticflow/tracker/evidence/TASK-0074/ for the human to read, AND state the before/after medians, the census counts and the chain depth VERBATIM in the closing History entry on this ticket - the History is what HEAD carries and what QA and the human grade against, the evidence file is not (it is gitignored and invisible to the detached worktree receipts run in, so no stored check may reference it). The baseline to beat is TASK-0062 measured, unchanged: /claims 1.714 s median warm, 23 round trips of which 9 are the readRowsByIds fan-out (now 3 batch waits), and a longest chain of 1 + 3 = four sequential waits through fetchPendingClaims -> readPendingObservations -> the fan-out. If the bar is missed, record the number and say so out loud; never move the bar, and never buy the difference by raising CHUNK_FANOUT without the measurement the description requires.
