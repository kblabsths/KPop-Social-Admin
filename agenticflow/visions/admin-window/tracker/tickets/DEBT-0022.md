# Three truth residuals on one class: a comment naming a defect that is fixed, a second spelling of 'unnamed source', and a count of 1 that reads as a plural

## Description
M3 endgame ruling (architect, 2026-09-11). Three residuals QA left unfiled across three lanes, folded into ONE ticket because each is a line or two and they are one class: a fact with two spellings, or a sentence describing a state that is no longer true. Their destinations are disjoint, so one builder session lands all three.

1. A STALE COMMENT THAT NAMES A LIVE DEFECT THAT IS FIXED (src/components/ui/window-line.tsx, the `scope` JSDoc around :800-808, from QA on BUG-0204). It says two of the six scan-arm call sites - /sources' trend and rejection lines - "are the same defect one page over, unfixed here and reported in this ticket's handoff rather than silently changed". That was true when BUG-0163 was in flight; /sources' window lines have since been fixed (BUG-0194 and the /sources lane). A comment claiming a live defect that no longer exists sends the next reader to look for it, and worse, reads as a licence to leave the arm alone.

2. THE SAME FACT SPELLED TWICE - is this source nameable? (src/lib/gauges/standing-disagreements.ts:134, from the TASK-0060 builder). `unnamedSources` counts `sourceById.get(sourceId) === undefined`, while every ROW of the same gauge gets its label from `sourceLabel`/`sourceNamesOf` in `src/lib/sources/names.ts`, the one owner of that question (`src/lib/db/review-item.ts:307` states the rule: never a `??` spelled here). Two definitions of "unnamed" in one gauge is the shape LESSONS 5 and 11 describe; the blank-source-name class already cost five tickets (BUG-0152/54/56/58/59) and the strategist ruled no sixth PER-SITE bug - this is the class site, fixed once, at the owner.

3. A COUNT OF 1 READS AS A PLURAL (src/components/ui/window-line.tsx:941, `${count(info.held)} ${of} in all`, and the same phrase at src/app/queues/page.tsx:506; QA residual on BUG-0197, pre-existing). One claim over a window renders "1 claims in all". The noun arrives from the caller, so the agreement has to be decided where the phrase is composed. The WORDS stay the designer's - this ticket changes agreement, not voice, and invents no new sentence.
