# The bound-ceiling sentence reads as exhaustion — "This view shows no further rows" beside a window line that says rows are not shown

## Description
PROVENANCE FIRST, so nobody over-reads this: the bound-ceiling arm was NOT reached in a browser. MAX_PAGE_OFFSET is 100,000 and the window is 50, so reaching it costs 2,000 presses and 100,000 DOM rows; QA graded it offline on TASK-0069/BUG-0172 for the same reason. The two sentences below are read from src/components/ui/paging.tsx on the walked tree, and the contradiction is between two strings whose rendering conditions the same file states. Everything else in this walk was measured in a real browser on a production build against staging.

THE TWO SENTENCES.

  limit arm      [data-paging="limit"]       "This view shows no further rows."
  exhausted arm  [data-paging="exhausted"]   "All claims in this view are shown."

They mean opposite things and read the same. Exhaustion is "the read established the set has ended"; the limit arm is "this app will not serve a bound past its ceiling, and rows you have not seen are still behind it". An operator who reaches the ceiling reads "This view shows no further rows", concludes the set is finished, and stops — which is the false-totality claim ARCHITECTURE §4.3 exists to make impossible ("A concatenation is still not a total"). The two sentences are also the only difference on screen: both arms draw no control, both render in the same secondary sans, in the same position.

The app is internally inconsistent about it at the same instant, by design: BUG-0172 criterion 6 ruled that at the bound ceiling the window line "still says rows are not shown, because they are" — MEASURED shape, from the /claims line one press earlier: "877 claims in all; the 850 longest-waiting are below — the rest are not shown." So the line says rows are held back and the control says the view shows no further rows. The line is right. The control's sentence is the one that misleads.

WHAT IT ALSO FAILS TO DO. Copy bar 3: "Every error names WHAT FAILED, THEN WHAT TO DO, with no apology." The ceiling is the app refusing, and paging.tsx's own docstring says why the arm exists ("without this arm the surface would draw a control every press is refused for, forever"). The sentence names neither the ceiling nor anything the operator can do about it — and there IS something: narrow the view with a bucket, source or domain facet and page the smaller set, which is how /claims is meant to be worked anyway.

Register: "plain, specific" (LOOK_AND_FEEL, The Voice). "This view shows no further rows" is neither — it is the passive form of a fact about the app stated as a fact about the data.

RULES: LOOK_AND_FEEL copy bar 3, The Voice register, and ARCHITECTURE §4.3 kind 3 ("refused with the reason named — never clamped in silence": a reason named in the server's answer and then not said on screen is the same silence one layer up). Traces to VISION: "real staging rows whose numbers match what the database says".

NOT IN SCOPE: the ceiling's value, the arm's ORDER (exhausted is read first and that ordering is load-bearing, BUG-0168), the hooks, and the window line at the ceiling (correct per BUG-0172 criterion 6 — do not change it).

Fix the words, not the machinery. Whoever takes it may reach the arm the way QA did, offline through the driver, rather than 2,000 presses.

## Acceptance criteria
1. The bound-ceiling sentence and the exhausted sentence cannot be confused: the limit arm does not claim the view holds no further rows, and its rendered text differs from the exhausted arm's in what it ASSERTS, not only in wording.
2. The limit arm names what stopped the paging — this app's own bound ceiling, not the end of the data — and names one thing the operator can do next (narrowing the view is the one this app offers). Copy bar 3: what failed, then what to do, no apology.
3. The exhausted arm is untouched — BOTH of its sentences. BUG-0180 lands ahead of this ticket and makes that arm's sentence conditional: the state whose two reads AGREE keeps "All {holds} in this view are shown." and the state whose reads DIVERGE says only what the read established. This ticket changes neither, changes no derivation behind them, and leaves the limit arm drawing no control, carrying data-paging="limit", in the same type step and position, still standing beside a refusal line when one is present.
4. The window line at the ceiling is untouched: it still says rows are not shown, with data-window-truncated="true" (BUG-0172 criterion 6), and the two now agree.
5. Both arms are asserted on rendered markup through the real driver at a bound past MAX_PAGE_OFFSET, offline — not by 2,000 presses in a browser.
6. No arm is added or removed; offered, loading, refused and exhausted render exactly what they render today.
7. (human-checkable) Human-check: read the limit arm's sentence beside the exhausted arm's. A stranger must be able to tell "the data ran out" from "this app will not fetch further" without consulting a document.
8. Lint is clean and a PRODUCTION build is green.

9. THE CONTROL'S TERMINAL SENTENCE NEVER RESTATES THE WINDOW LINE'S CLOSING CLAUSE (architect ruling 2026-09-11, from this ticket's own builder note: in the diverged state the line ends "the read found no more." and the new control sentence reads "The read returned no further claims." — the stutter `src/components/ui/window-line.tsx`'s own docstring warns about, "two sentences about one question read as a page arguing with itself when they differ and as a stutter when they agree", LESSONS 11). You write the terminal sentences LAST — that is the whole reason this ticket is third on the lane — so holding the rule for all three of them is yours: in every state of a paged surface, the sentence the control renders and the clause the window line ENDS ON say DIFFERENT things. The line's closing clause is the WINDOW's verdict (what the read came back with); the control's terminal sentence is the CONTROL's (what a press would now do, or that this app has stopped asking). Neither is a restatement of the other, and neither answers the other's question a second time.

Graded STRUCTURALLY, never by pinning a word — the words are the designer's (bar: The Voice) and a rewording must move the test with it. A named test renders ONE paged surface through the real driver (one `PagingProvider`, the line and the control together, the way `tests/offline/ui/paging.test.ts` already renders them at its claims case) and reads both strings off the markup: the line's closing clause is taken from the rendered line, the control's terminal sentence from the rendered `[data-paging]` arm, and neither string contains the other. Asserted in the four states the surface reaches: the agreeing state and the diverged state of BUG-0180's exhausted arm, the still-offering state, and this ticket's own bound ceiling. Nothing here pins a sentence, forbids a word, or grades whether the wording is GOOD — that is the designer's walk (criterion 7). This grades only that one screen does not answer one question twice.
